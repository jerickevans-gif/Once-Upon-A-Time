Once Upon A Time Newsletter

December 2025

Written by: Sydney Phelps  |  Published 1/18/2026

As another year draws to a close, we want to thank the families, students, and supporters who made 2025 such a meaningful season for Once Upon a Time. From our first winter jazz showcase to a full calendar of community performances, this has been a year of growth, creativity, and connection.

Our studios in Queens welcomed more young performers than ever before. Across dance, theater, music, and visual art, children stepped onto the stage, discovered new skills, and shared their stories with the families and neighbors who cheered them on.

This issue, we spotlight the community garden that has become a gathering place for our students and their families, share an update on its winter harvest, and celebrate one of the volunteers whose dedication keeps our programs thriving.

Looking ahead, spring enrollment opens soon for our seasonal camps and private lessons. Whether your child is returning or joining us for the first time, there is a place for them in our classes.

Thank you for being part of our story. None of this work would be possible without the generosity of our donors and the time given so freely by our volunteers and instructors.

Community Garden

Written by: Sydney Phelps  |  Published 1/18/2026

Tucked beside our studio, the community garden has become one of our most beloved spaces. Each week, children and families gather to plant, weed, and harvest together, learning patience and teamwork with their hands in the soil.

The garden is more than a place to grow vegetables. It is where neighbors meet, where younger students learn from older ones, and where the lessons of our arts programs — creativity, care, and collaboration — take root in a new form.

Community Garden Update

Written by: Sydney Phelps  |  Published 1/18/2026

This season, our young gardeners brought in their first winter harvest — hardy greens, herbs, and the last of the autumn tomatoes. The children proudly shared the produce with their families, and a portion was donated to a local food pantry.

With help from volunteers, we have added raised beds and a small toolshed, making the garden easier for our youngest participants to tend. Come spring, we will plant a pollinator patch and expand the herb garden the children use in their cooking sessions.

Volunteer Spotlight

Written by: Sydney Phelps  |  Published 1/18/2026

This month we celebrate Marcus, a volunteer who has given his Saturday mornings to Once Upon a Time for the past three years. From setting up the stage before performances to mentoring our older students, his steady presence has touched every corner of our programs.

“Watching a shy kid find their voice on stage never gets old,” he says. Volunteers like Marcus are the heart of our community — if you would like to join him, we would love to hear from you.
